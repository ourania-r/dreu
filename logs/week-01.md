## Goals

- Begin the first assigned task of the program: gather research papers at the intersection of large language models (LLMs), cryptography and post-quantum cryptography (PQC), and software engineering, to build a foundational understanding of the research area.
- Start with the foundational and lab-specific papers to anchor the rest of the reading.

## Approach and Implementation

Before the program officially began, Dr. Zhang and I held an introductory video call on June 2 (1:30 to 2:30 PM) to introduce ourselves and sort out program logistics, which we also turned into an expectations meeting. We covered scheduling, my start date, the research topic, and expectations for the summer. Due to mutually shared schedule conflicts and commitments, we agreed on a remote and asynchronous working arrangement, communicating through a dedicated Slack channel. The plan for my first phase: build a foundation in the research area through the literature, then demonstrate that understanding to Dr. Zhang before moving into the research itself.

This week I began the literature search, covering papers from 2022 through mid-2026 with emphasis on 2024 to 2026. I searched arXiv, IEEE Xplore, and the ACM Digital Library, drawing from venues including IEEE S&P, USENIX Security, CCS, ICSE, QCE, SANER, and EMNLP, plus NIST publications. My selection criterion was direct relevance to at least two of the three core themes: LLMs, cryptography/PQC, and software engineering/security. I anchored the reading in Dr. Zhang's EST Lab work first: the IBM Db2 quantum-safe migration case study, the survey of PQC support in cryptographic libraries, and the AQuA framework vision paper, alongside foundational material (the Bernstein and Lange PQC survey, Shor's algorithm, and the NIST FIPS 203/204/205 executive summaries).
